Two-day process analysis

As-is, to-be and a time estimate.

“Only Kim knows how it works.”

Everything we build starts here. We spend two days with the people who do the work and follow real cases, from the first email to the invoice. Not the process in the manual, the one that actually runs, with its handovers, waiting and retyping.

You get two drawings and a number. The first shows how the work runs today. The second shows how it could run, with each step given to the right lane: AI, fixed rules or a person. The number is our estimate of the hours you get back, and what it takes to build.

Sometimes the answer is a better checklist, not software. Then we say so.

If the two days don’t turn up anything worth fixing, you don’t pay for them.

2

Sales automation

Routing, follow-up and CRM hygiene.

“New leads sit in an inbox all weekend.”

Most of the time lost in sales isn’t selling. It’s working out who should take an enquiry, chasing the details that were missing, and remembering to follow up on the quote that went quiet.

We set up rules that do this for you. New enquiries go to the right person straight away. The customer gets a first reply that asks only for what they haven’t told you yet. Quiet quotes get a reminder, and duplicates and empty fields are cleaned up as they happen.

This is mostly rules, not AI, and it runs in the systems you already use. You can read the rules and change them yourself.

3

AI sales assistants

Research, call prep and first drafts.

“Our sellers spend the morning researching instead of calling.”

This is where AI earns its place: reading a lot and writing a first version. Before the first call of the day, each seller has a short brief on the account. After the call, the notes are summarised and logged in the CRM with the next steps.

Replies and follow-ups arrive as drafts. A seller reads them, edits what needs editing, and sends them. Nothing goes to a customer without a person deciding it should.

4

Quotes and proposals

Priced proposals the same day.

“Quotes take two days because the pricing lives in one person’s head.”

A quote is often the slowest step in the whole sale, because the prices, discounts and exceptions live in one person’s head. We help that person write them down as rules, in one place.

From there, a quote is drafted from the call notes, the email or the drawings. A seller checks it and sends it, and the ERP is updated when it goes out. The person who knows the prices still sets the rules and handles the exceptions; they just stop typing the same quote again.

5

Data and integrations

CRM, ERP, email and calendar in sync.

“Every order gets typed in twice.”

When systems don’t talk to each other, people do the talking for them, by copying. Orders are read straight from the email or PDF and proposed in the ERP, ready for someone to approve. The CRM fills in as calls, emails and orders happen, not on Friday afternoon.

We work with the common CRMs (HubSpot, Salesforce, Pipedrive, Microsoft Dynamics), Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace, Slack and Teams. If you run something else, we’ll tell you honestly whether we’re a good fit.
