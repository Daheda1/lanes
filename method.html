Introduction

Most sales teams don't have an AI problem. They have a process nobody has ever drawn.

Information lives in five places. Handoffs depend on someone remembering. Quotes get retyped from an email into a spreadsheet and from the spreadsheet into a Word template, and everyone quietly keeps their own list on the side.

New tools often make this worse when they're bolted on top. You get another tab and another login, and the underlying mess is still there.

So we start by drawing the process. Not the version in the handbook, the one that actually happens. We draw it as a swimlane: one lane per role, with every step placed in the lane of whoever does it. Once everyone can see it, the fixes are usually obvious, and so is what they're worth. Want to try it first? Here’s how to draw your own swimlane in an afternoon.

2 Two days with your people

The analysis takes two days, with the people who do the work. Not a steering group, and not a questionnaire.

Day 1
We walk through real cases with each role. We follow a handful of recent deals or orders from first contact to invoice, and draw the as-is as we go.
Day 2
We go through the drawing with the whole group, find the bottlenecks together and sketch the to-be. In the last hour we walk a decision-maker through both.

Who should be there:

  • A couple of sellers, ideally one experienced and one newer.
  • Sales ops or customer service, whoever picks up after the deal is won.
  • Whoever owns pricing, quotes or invoicing.
  • A decision-maker for the last hour of day two.

Nobody needs to be in the room for two full days. Most people need a few hours.

3 What you get

Shortly after the two days, you get a short report built around three things.

The deliverable

  1. 01
    The as-is swimlaneYour process as it runs today, with every role, system, handoff and wait.
  2. 02
    The to-be swimlaneA concrete suggestion for how it should run, with each step moved into the right lane.
  3. 03
    A time estimateThe hours the to-be saves per week or per case, and how long it takes to build.

The estimate is the part most people care about, so we're careful with it. It's based on what we saw over the two days, not on vendor benchmarks, and we show how we got to the number so you can check it. Read how we estimate the time you’ll get back.

4 Done properly

The person who sits with your team is the person who draws the swimlanes and builds the solution. Nothing gets lost between a sales meeting and a delivery team, because there isn't one.

Every estimate comes with the reasoning behind it. If part of a process can't be automated well, we say so, even when it means less work for us.

One process done properly beats five done halfway.

And if the two days don't turn up anything worth fixing, you don't pay for them. You keep the as-is drawing either way.

5 If you go ahead

Most of the time, the to-be is worth building. You can build it with us, or take the drawings and the estimate and do it yourselves. Both are fine.

When we build, it runs inside the tools your team already lives in: your CRM, your inbox, Slack or Teams. We work in short cycles, and a first working version is used on real cases early, so feedback is about reality rather than a demo.

We're deliberate about where AI goes. Language models are excellent at reading, summarising and drafting. They are the wrong tool for anything that has to be exactly right every time, like pricing, routing or compliance checks. Those stay as plain, predictable rules you can read and change. More on that in When AI is the wrong tool.

6 Working together

We're a small, senior team, and you talk directly to the people who draw your swimlanes and build the result.
