Introduction

The time estimate is the number people care about most. So it should be the number you can check most easily.

Most estimates in this industry come from somewhere else: a vendor’s case study, an industry report, a slide that says “save up to 40%”. They might be true for someone. They tell you very little about your team.

Ours come from what we see during the two days, in your process, with your people. And we show the working, so you can follow every step from what we observed to the number at the bottom.

2 What we count

For every step in the as-is swimlane, we write down four things. None of them need special tools. They need someone to ask, watch and write it down.

MeasureWhat it meansHow we get it
Touch timeMinutes someone actually works on the stepWatching real cases, then asking
FrequencyHow often the step happensCounts from your CRM or inbox
Waiting timeHow long the work sits before the next stepTimestamps on recent cases
ReworkHow often it has to be done againAsking the people who fix it

Touch time is what turns into hours saved. Waiting time is what turns into faster answers for your customers. We keep them apart, because they’re worth different things to you and they’re easy to mix up.

3 A worked example

Here’s how the numbers come together for a typical quote process. The figures are an example, not a client’s, but they’re the kind of numbers we usually see in a small sales team sending around 25 quotes a week.

4 Ranges, not promises

Two days is enough to see a process clearly. It isn’t enough to time every step to the minute. So we never give a single number. In the example above, we’d write 15 to 20 hours a week, and explain what would push it towards either end.

When we recommend whether to build something, we use the low end. If a lane is only worth it at the optimistic end of the range, it isn’t worth it, and we’ll say so.

If the low end isn’t worth it, the lane isn’t worth building.

We also say what the estimate leaves out. Faster quotes often win more deals, but that’s hard to prove in advance, so we mention it and don’t count it.

5 What it costs to get there

Time saved only matters next to what it costs to save it. Every estimate comes with the other side of the scale:

  • Building it. How many days the to-be takes to build and test with your team.
  • Running it. Licences, integrations and AI usage per month, if any.
  • Keeping it running. Our time after launch for fixes and changes.

6 Checking afterwards

An estimate you never check is just a sales pitch. Because we wrote down the as-is numbers during the two days, we have a baseline. A few weeks after launch we measure the same steps again, with the same method, and compare.

If the result is below the range, we find out why and fix it. That’s part of staying after launch, not an extra.
